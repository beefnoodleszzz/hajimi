## Voiceover

> **Assume a magical, instantaneous halt: Earth’s rotation stops for exactly one second.**
>
> The ground would stop — but you wouldn’t.
>
> At the equator, Earth’s surface is moving east at about **465 meters per second**, nearly 1,000 miles per hour. Your body, the atmosphere, and the oceans would keep that sideways motion.
>
> In one second, that is roughly **465 meters of relative movement** — under this simplified model.
>
> The poles are different: their rotational speed is close to zero. You would not fly into space: gravity would still hold you to Earth.
>
> If the planet restarted, the mismatch between land, air, water, and everything unattached would be catastrophic.
>
> Its spin also bends global winds and ocean currents through the Coriolis effect.
>
> So what moves first: you, the atmosphere, or the ocean?
